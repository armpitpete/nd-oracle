# ND Oracle topic expansion backlog v0.2

Status date: 2026-08-12

Base authoritative corpus: five concepts only — neurodiversity, autism, ADHD, executive function and sensory processing.

This document is a planning backlog. Inclusion here does **not** make a topic authoritative, reviewed, accepted or publishable. Every topic must still pass the normal evidence, uncertainty, perspective, provenance and human-acceptance process.

## Selection rule

Prefer batches that:

1. close obvious coverage gaps around the existing corpus;
2. have clear user value across childhood and adulthood;
3. have enough authoritative and peer-reviewed material to support a bounded seed without pretending consensus where it does not exist;
4. add useful relations to existing concepts;
5. avoid duplicating concepts that are better treated as cross-cutting experiences;
6. can be researched in parallel so one disputed source cannot stall the whole site.

## Batch A — next foundational conditions

These five should be researched together as the next expansion batch.

| Priority | Candidate topic | Why it belongs in the first batch | Main boundary to protect |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dyslexia | Major specific learning difficulty; clear lifespan and education/work relevance; creates a needed learning-differences branch beside ADHD/autism | do not equate reading difficulty with low intelligence; separate dyslexia from intellectual/learning disability |
| 2 | Developmental co-ordination disorder (DCD / dyspraxia) | Adds motor-coordination and everyday access needs absent from the current graph; relates naturally to executive and sensory demands | distinguish developmental DCD from acquired dyspraxia and from generic clumsiness |
| 3 | Tourette syndrome and tic disorders | Adds a neurological/neurodevelopmental branch with strong overlap questions around ADHD/OCD and common public misconceptions | Tourette is not synonymous with swearing; distinguish tics, chronic tic disorders and Tourette syndrome |
| 4 | Learning disability / intellectual disability | Essential to avoid a site centred only on neurodivergent people without intellectual disability; material implications for communication, health access and support | UK “learning disability” and international “intellectual disability” terminology must be handled explicitly; do not collapse this into specific learning difficulties |
| 5 | Developmental language disorder (DLD) | Adds a major lifelong communication-related neurodevelopmental condition and helps prevent autism/ADHD becoming catch-all explanations for language difficulties | distinguish DLD from language difference, bilingualism, hearing loss, autism and acquired language disorders |

### Public-source discovery anchors

- NHS: dyslexia in children and adults.
- NHS: developmental co-ordination disorder / dyspraxia in children and adults.
- NHS: Tourette syndrome.
- NHS / NHS England: learning disabilities and the distinction from learning difficulties.
- NHS speech-and-language services: developmental language disorder.
- NICE ADHD guidance: explicitly recognises other neurodevelopmental disorders including autism, tic disorders, learning disability and specific learning difficulties.
- NICE autism guidance: explicitly asks clinicians to consider learning disability, DCD/motor coordination, literacy/numeracy learning problems, speech/language disorders and tics/Tourette among coexisting conditions.

These are discovery anchors, not a substitute for a full evidence pack.

## Batch B — learning, communication and motor concepts requiring taxonomy work

These are valuable but should follow Batch A because their boundaries and terminology need more deliberate modelling.

| Candidate | Reason to include | Why not Batch A |
|---|---|---|
| Dyscalculia / mathematics learning disorder | fills numeracy-specific gap and complements dyslexia | terminology and diagnostic boundaries vary; source mapping needed first |
| Speech sound disorder | important communication/access topic with developmental relevance | must be separated carefully from DLD and acquired speech disorders |
| Auditory processing constructs | frequently invoked by users and services | construct and diagnostic boundaries are contested across disciplines |
| Dysgraphia / written-expression difficulties | common user-facing term with practical education/work consequences | needs taxonomy review because usage is inconsistent across systems |

## Batch C — cross-cutting experiences and mechanisms

These should not be treated automatically as diagnoses. Each needs its own evidence-status decision.

| Candidate | Initial treatment |
|---|---|
| Masking / camouflaging | cross-cutting experience; evidence-rich enough to investigate, but definitions and measurement vary |
| Autistic burnout | lived-experience-led construct; preserve origin, community evidence and uncertainty |
| Interoception | mechanism/experience concept spanning multiple neurotypes; avoid overclaiming causal explanations |
| Emotional regulation | cross-cutting functional concept; distinguish description from moral judgement and diagnostic shorthand |
| Sensory overload / shutdown / meltdown | user-facing experience terms; define carefully and avoid pretending one mechanism explains every event |
| Monotropism | theoretical framework/perspective, not settled universal mechanism |
| Demand avoidance / PDA | contested terminology and nosology; requires explicit disagreement mapping before any public seed |
| Rejection sensitivity / RSD | popular term with uneven formal status; should begin as a claim-and-uncertainty investigation, not a condition page |

## Batch D — support and life-context concepts

Once the condition graph is broader, ND Oracle should add concepts that answer practical questions rather than only “what condition is this?”

Candidates:

- reasonable adjustments and accommodations;
- communication access and AAC;
- executive-demand redesign;
- sensory environment design;
- education access;
- workplace access;
- sleep and neurodivergence;
- transitions and change;
- co-occurrence / multiple diagnoses;
- diagnostic assessment and its limits.

## Explicit non-goals for the expansion lane

- Do not create pages merely because a term is popular online.
- Do not give every candidate equal evidential status.
- Do not turn a single book, paper, charity page or guideline into the centre of a topic.
- Do not resolve contested terminology by silently choosing one camp.
- Do not infer individual diagnosis or treatment recommendations.
- Do not delay the whole batch because one source or one historical attribution is unresolved.

## Batch execution model

For Batch A, work in parallel:

1. define scope and exclusions for all five topics;
2. assemble authoritative guidance plus high-quality review/primary evidence for all five;
3. create claim/uncertainty/perspective matrices for all five;
4. map relations to the existing five concepts;
5. adversarially test the strongest claims and obvious misconceptions;
6. produce five candidate concept objects;
7. validate the batch as a whole;
8. present the human acceptance decision in ordinary language;
9. only after acceptance, promote authoritative objects and add public pages together.

## Recommended next substantive lane

**Batch A: dyslexia + DCD/dyspraxia + Tourette/tic disorders + learning disability/intellectual disability + developmental language disorder.**

This is large enough to materially improve the site, small enough to review coherently, and broad enough that ND Oracle stops looking like an autism/ADHD site with three supporting concepts.
