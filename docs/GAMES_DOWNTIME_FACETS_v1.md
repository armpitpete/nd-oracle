# Games & downtime descriptive facets v1

Date: 2026-09-02

## Purpose

ND Oracle does not classify games as 'for autism', 'for ADHD', 'neurodivergent games', 'calming games' or therapeutic interventions merely because users or storefront tags describe them that way. Games are routed by observable play and access characteristics that a reader can inspect.

Steam may be used for candidate discovery and for developer/publisher-supplied product facts. User tags such as `Relaxing` or `Cozy` are not, by themselves, governed evidence of fit or emotional effect.

## Facets

Each reviewed game should record relevant information, when supported, across these dimensions using the existing Resource fields until a schema change is justified:

1. **Time pressure** — timers, clocks, forced pacing, real-time deadlines.
2. **Reaction demand** — whether rapid responses or repeated real-time precision are required.
3. **Failure consequences** — fail states, punishment, lost progress, finite resources, scores.
4. **Interruption tolerance** — pause, save/resume, short-session design, idle/background modes.
5. **Input complexity** — number and simultaneity of controls, pointing/dragging, remapping and assistive-input support.
6. **Sensory demand** — motion, flashes, camera movement, visual clutter, audio layering and available controls. Do not collapse this to one universal score.
7. **Cognitive/strategic load** — planning, optimisation, working-memory, spatial or puzzle demands.
8. **Social demand** — required multiplayer, matchmaking, voice/text chat, strangers, team dependence and optional sharing.
9. **Mode dependence** — whether sandbox, creative, mission, survival, multiplayer or other modes materially change the above facets.
10. **Structure of interest** — building, sorting, collecting, repetition, exploration or other mechanics described directly without diagnosis stereotypes.

## Evidence rule

Prefer developer/publisher documentation and dedicated accessibility documentation for mechanics and settings. Specialist accessibility reviews can add independently observed access detail when clearly attributed. Storefront user tags and popularity are discovery signals only.

If a source says a game is relaxing, soothing, cozy or stress-free, ND Oracle may record the concrete mechanics underneath that description but must not turn the adjective into a universal outcome claim.

## Boundaries

- Resource inclusion is not endorsement.
- Game Resources remain `claims: []` unless a serious testable efficacy or safety proposition is deliberately introduced with governed Evidence.
- A preference for repetition, sorting, collecting, building, solitary play or low pressure must not be used as diagnostic evidence.
- 'Simple controls' means only the bounded documented control fact; it does not imply comprehensive motor accessibility.
- 'Single-player' means no required multiplayer for the described mode; it does not imply low cognitive, sensory or emotional demand.
- Aesthetic genre labels such as cozy cannot override documented timers, scores, missions, combat or failure states.

## Current bounded coverage

The v1 expansion uses 13 governed game Resources: the existing Unpacking, Minecraft and Stardew Valley records plus Tiny Glade, Townscaper, SUMMERHOUSE, Gourdlets, Garden Galaxy, The Shape of Things, Cats Organized Neatly, Dorfromantik, Minami Lane and Cloud Gardens.

The public Games & downtime group expands from one Question to twelve Questions covering time pressure, reaction speed, interruption, mode-configurable pressure, control complexity, failure consequences, single-player/social load, building/sorting/collecting mechanics, gaming-community social load, checking accessibility features before purchase, adult autistic gaming communities and the still-open sensory-demand gap.

## Supporting accessibility and community routes

Steam's accessibility-feature taxonomy is useful governed inspection metadata because it exposes developer-declared gameplay, visual, audio and input features and supports store filtering. It is not an independent accessibility certification, and missing tags do not prove a feature is absent.

The adult-community seed is deliberately narrow: Incompatible Cartridges is a current CAAS online group for autistic adults aged 18+ who live in Harrow or Hillingdon. It closes the previous absence of any governed adult gaming-community route but does not close UK-wide adult-community coverage or establish low social demand.

## Completion boundary

This is a bounded useful-coverage milestone, not an attempt to catalogue Steam. Future additions should be driven by unanswered Questions or materially new facet coverage rather than by catalogue size.
