# ND Oracle Terminology Accessibility Policy v1

Status: **OWNER-REFINEMENT CANDIDATE**  
Date: **2026-09-28**

## Rule

> **Technical or unfamiliar terms should be understandable where the reader first encounters them and have one canonical glossary entry.**

This is a reading-access rule, not a new evidence authority. Terminology help must not create, strengthen or weaken governed clinical, efficacy or safety claims.

## Two-location model

1. **Glossary:** every approved terminology item has one canonical, stable glossary entry.
2. **First meaningful use:** on Topic, Question and Resource reading pages, the term is explained immediately after the first paragraph in which understanding it materially helps the reader.

A term is explained no more than once per page. Several terms first used in the same paragraph are grouped into one terminology component rather than stacked as separate boxes.

## Component contract

Every entry includes:
- canonical term;
- aliases and/or acronym expansion when applicable;
- visible component parts;
- a short meaning for each part;
- **What it means here** — the meaning of the whole term;
- a link to the canonical glossary entry;
- a related ND Oracle route;
- review date and provenance in the registry.

The same visual grammar is used for word roots, acronyms and technical phrases. The chunks do **not** all represent etymology.

## Safety and accuracy rules

- Do not invent word roots.
- Word parts are a learning and memory aid, not a formal definition.
- Acronyms are expanded rather than treated as etymology.
- Technical phrases are broken into meaningful phrase components.
- Where safe decomposition would mislead, the whole technical word is used as a single explained chunk.
- The whole-term meaning is mandatory.
- Contested terminology must say that it is contested where material.
- Community terminology must not be presented as a formal diagnosis unless it is one.
- Aliases resolve to one canonical glossary entry.
- Organisation names, product names, navigation labels, metadata, citations and source titles do not trigger first-use boxes merely because they contain a term.

## Qualification threshold

Include a term when at least one applies:
- specialist neurodiversity terminology;
- clinical or diagnostic terminology;
- research-method terminology;
- an unfamiliar acronym;
- a technical phrase whose specialist meaning is not obvious from ordinary language;
- a term whose ordinary-language reading could mislead.

Ordinary words, product names, organisation names and self-explanatory phrases are excluded.

## Current bounded audit

The v1 audit contains **52 canonical entries**: the 17 previously prepared term guides plus 35 additional terms found in the current 366-object governed corpus.

Future terms use the same qualification rule; 52 is the starting audit, not a permanent ceiling.

## Navigation and route authority

The public Glossary route is internally linked but remains **noindex, follow** and outside the accepted 450-route sitemap until a later protected production route-count reconciliation explicitly authorises a canonical-route change.

Find links directly to the Glossary for readers who already know a technical term, but glossary entries do **not** enter the governed discovery ranking in v1. This preserves the accepted discovery authority and avoids converting terminology help into a new ranked knowledge route.

## Editorial workflow

When adding a qualifying term:
1. add or amend the canonical registry entry;
2. give it a unique stable ID;
3. record aliases and acronym/full form where needed;
4. explain the visible parts;
5. write the plain whole-term meaning;
6. link at least one relevant ND Oracle route;
7. run terminology validation and the full site regression suite;
8. inspect first-use placement and narrow-layout rendering.
