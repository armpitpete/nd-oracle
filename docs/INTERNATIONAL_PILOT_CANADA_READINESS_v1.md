# International source-readiness probe — Canada v1

Date: 2026-09-05
Status: readiness pass
Parent architecture: `docs/INTERNATIONAL_EXPANSION_ARCHITECTURE_v1.md`

## Decision

**PASS — Canada supports a bounded federal-orientation + provincial implementation Assessment & diagnosis package.**

The initial package contains:
1. Canada-wide autism-assessment orientation;
2. an explicitly Ontario-only child-autism assessment route.

## Why Canada is architecturally useful

Canada provides a stronger federal/provincial test than the first two packages. Federal public-health information can orient the assessment journey, while practical delivery and programme routes can be provincial. Ontario's diagnostic hubs are therefore a useful positive control for **subnational non-inheritance**.

## Source readiness

### Federal orientation

Public Health Agency of Canada guidance says people with concerns should speak to a healthcare provider about autism assessment; providers may refer to specialists, and specialist/team assessment can establish diagnosis.

Source:
`https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/diseases/autism-spectrum-disorder-asd/signs-characteristics.html`

Boundary: federal guidance does not establish one Canada-wide diagnostic-service delivery pathway.

### Ontario implementation

Ontario publishes child/youth autism assessment and diagnosis information, including qualified professionals and five diagnostic hubs.

Source:
`https://www.ontario.ca/page/autism-assessment-diagnosis`

The Ontario page was updated 10 March 2026. Current 2026–27 provincial service objectives also describe five ASD diagnostic hubs serving children and youth under 18.

Boundary: Ontario services and programme rules remain Ontario-specific.

## Language

The bounded sources used here were reviewed in English. Canada has official-language obligations and future material may require French-source review. The international translation rule remains authoritative: machine translation can assist research but does not become provenance by itself.

## Freshness ownership

- federal assessment orientation: normal authoritative-guidance cadence;
- Ontario diagnostic hubs/programme requirements: shorter provincial implementation cadence;
- any additional province/territory package must name its own critical sources and review triggers.

## Readiness verdict

Canada passes the no-empty-country rule and adds a genuinely different architecture proof: national orientation plus explicit provincial implementation. No placeholder provinces or mass-country expansion are authorised.
