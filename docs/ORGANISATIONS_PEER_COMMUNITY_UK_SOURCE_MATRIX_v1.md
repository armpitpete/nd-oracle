## Geographic proof set

- **York / Yorkshire:** BDA's network exposes Yorkshire association coverage; NAS remains a local-search fallback. ND Oracle does not invent a York-specific peer recommendation.
- **Cumbria:** Dyspraxia Foundation lists a Cumbria local group.
- **Highland:** ARGH Scotland is Highland-rooted and autistic-led.
- **Powys:** Disability Wales' DPO list includes Accessibility Powys.
- **Fermanagh:** Autism NI's broader support information explicitly includes Fermanagh among Northern Ireland communities; the PACE peer-network route retains its own eligibility.

## Research evidence used for design, not endorsement

Crompton CJ, Hallett S, McAuliffe C, Stanfield AC, Fletcher-Watson S. *“A Group of Fellow Travellers Who Understand”: Interviews With Autistic People About Post-diagnostic Peer Support in Adulthood.* Frontiers in Psychology. 2022;13:831628. DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2022.831628.

The qualitative UK study involved 12 autistic adults and identified both perceived value and practical limits: flexible formats, sensory and accessibility considerations, facilitator training and support, sustainability, intersectionality and a boundary with specialist services. The sample is too small and specific to support a universal efficacy claim or to certify any listed community.

## Excluded / bounded candidates

- Commercial professional directories are not used as proof of peer community quality.
- A cross-ND directory can be listed only with its commercial and self-described completeness limits visible.
- Social-media groups using an organisation's name without verified affiliation are not treated as official groups.
- Local groups with no stable first-party identity are not copied into Oracle merely to increase counts.
